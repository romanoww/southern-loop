Campervan road trip · Jan – Jul 2027

The Southern Loop

New Zealand, both islands, into Australia's east coast, then Japan — from Christchurch's alpine south up through the Bay of Islands, Melbourne to Port Douglas along the coast, and a Hokkaido campervan loop into fixed-stay Honshu. Built with 2022–2026 historical climate averages.

Trip notes & methodology

Each time slot shows the 5-year average with the actual observed range beneath it (every night of the stay × 2022–2026). “Recorded” is the coldest and hottest reading seen in that window — plan for those, not the average. Tongariro uses National Park Village (1043m), as the Whakapapa grid cell sits on the volcano slopes and read ~4°C too cold. Alpine valley lows may still read ~1°C warm. Stays are tagged 🔌 full hookup, 🚐 self-contained (Green Warrant required) or ⛺ basic/DOC, with facility codes: P power, D dump, S showers, L laundry, K kitchen, G playground, W wifi. Red flags mark sites needing advance booking or a ballot. Campsites and activities are suggestions, not bookings — availability, prices, seasonal closures and track conditions all change, so verify each one before you rely on it. Hikes are capped at ~3h and most are under 2h; “half day” entries are boat- or drive-based rather than walking. Campervan legs are all of New Zealand and Melbourne→Byron Bay; the Gold Coast, Noosa and Port Douglas stops assume fixed accommodation. From 7 June 2026 a Green Warrant (fixed toilet) is required to freedom camp where self-containment is mandated — confirm your rental carries one. Activities are tagged for camper access: no badge means you can drive the van to the trailhead, 🚐 tight flags a small or gravel car park, ⛴ means boat or shuttle only. Whale timing matters — the east-Australian humpback migration runs May to November and builds through June, so your earlier NSW stops are at the very start of it and several operators do not begin until ~1 June. Every campsite and activity name links to Google Maps — tap the name to open it, or 📍map beside the dates for the town itself. Map lines follow real routed roads. Drive times are estimates — routed distance combined with a speed model, validated against known real durations to within ~7% on average, and deliberately a little conservative since they don’t include stops. The winding Nelson Lakes–Tākaka and Punakaiki legs are the least certain. Tap “Drive” for live Google times. Japan: driving needs a 1949 Geneva Convention IDP — the ANWB issues both, so ask specifically for the 1949 booklet, not the 1968 Vienna one, which is invalid in Japan. Michi-no-eki roadside stations are free overnight parking but are rest stops, not campsites: one night, no tables or awnings. Not a booking tool.
